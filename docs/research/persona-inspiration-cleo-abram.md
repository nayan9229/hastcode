# Persona inspiration: Cleo Abram's explainer style

Researched 2026-10-22 to shape the voice of HastCode's house byline (see
`docs/editorial/house-byline-voice.md`). We borrow **style principles only**:
not her name, likeness, biography, catchphrases or show format.

## What her work is known for

- **Optimistic framing.** Huge If True is described as an optimistic show
  about science and technology, a counterweight to pessimistic coverage,
  asking "what could go right?" rather than "what could go wrong?"
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleo_Abram);
  [Semafor, 2025-06-13](https://www.semafor.com/article/06/13/2025/cleo-abram-on-techs-bright-future);
  [The Banner, 2024-06](https://www.thebanner.org/mixed-media/2024/06/cleo-abram-huge-if-true-youtube)).
  Note: much of this framing is her own self-description.
- **One innovation per episode, in depth** (Wikipedia; channel description
  as quoted by the sources above).
- **Visual first.** She looks for the one visual (chart, diagram,
  demonstration) that makes the idea understandable and lets it shape the
  story ([In Sync interview, videoconsortium.org](https://videoconsortium.org/mag/episode-8-cleo-abram)).
- **Rigor under speed pressure.** Emphasis on fact-checking and the tension
  between quality and publishing speed
  ([Levels podcast #195](https://levels.com/podcasts/195-video-content-creation-and-maintaining-journalistic-integrity-cleo-abram-ben-grynol)).
- **Staged pipeline.** Pitch, research doc, visual outline, then script.
  Secondary: an auto-generated summary of a podcast conversation, weight lightly
  ([chaindesk summary](https://chaindesk.ai/tools/youtube-summarizer/why-every-cleo-abram-video-goes-viral-qYLzSN_Jz_E)).
- **Background:** video journalist at Vox (host of Answered, co-host of Glad
  You Asked) before launching Huge If True in 2022 (Wikipedia).

## What we take for HastCode

| Her principle | HastCode translation |
|---|---|
| "What could go right?" | Every Explainer ends with what the architecture makes possible, after its trade-offs. |
| One innovation per episode | One architectural idea per Explainer (already the glossary definition). |
| Visual first | Pick the key Mermaid diagram before drafting; the diagram is the outline. |
| Fact-checking | First-party sources only, every claim linked (Editorial Policy). |
| Pitch → research doc → visual outline → script | Pitch → source notes → diagram outline → draft → verify. |

## What we do not take

Her name, face, voice, biography, credentials, show title or catchphrases.
The house byline is an original, disclosed persona.
