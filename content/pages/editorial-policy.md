This policy describes how every Explainer and Brief on Hast Code is made. It
applies to everything we publish, and the Author named on each piece is
accountable for it meeting this policy.

## Who is accountable

Every piece carries the byline of a real, named Author, linked to an Author
page with their bio and public profiles. We never publish under invented
names or personas, and an AI tool is never credited as an author. Hast Code

## Sources

- **First-party public sources only.** We cite the people who built or run
  the system: official documentation, specifications and RFCs, research
  papers, source code and changelogs, the company's own engineering blog, and
  talks given by its engineers. We do not build on other outlets' summaries,
  rumours, leaks or private information.
- **Every claim is linked.** Each factual claim, number and quotation links
  the first-party source that supports it, inline where it is made.
- **Every Explainer ends with its sources** in a Sources section. Every Brief
  links the primary source of the event it covers.
- **Reconstruction is labelled.** A Case Study reconstructs a system from its
  makers' public material. Where that material leaves a gap and we infer how
  something works, we say so in the text rather than present it as fact.

## AI assistance

Drafts may be written with the help of AI tools. A draft is only the start:
the Author researches every claim against its first-party source, checks
every number, diagram and code sample, rewrites what is wrong or unclear, and
decides whether the piece is published. Pieces drafted with AI assistance
carry this note:

> Drafted with AI assistance, researched, verified and edited by [Author].

where [Author] is the name of the Author on the byline. We publish only as
many pieces as the Author can verify this way.

## Dates and Review

Every Explainer shows when it was published and when it was last reviewed.
We Review every Explainer at least every 90 days: the Author re-reads it
against its sources and either updates it, which moves its "Last reviewed"
date, or confirms it is still right, which does not. A "Last reviewed" date
never moves without a real check. Briefs are dated commentary on a moment and
are not reviewed, but they are corrected.

## Corrections

If we get something wrong, we fix it and say so.

- Report an error through the [Contact](/contact/) page, with the page's
  address and, if you can, a first-party source.
- We check every report against the sources. If the piece is wrong, we
  correct it as soon as we can.
- A correction that changes a fact, number or conclusion is noted at the end
  of the piece with the date and what changed. Fixes to typos, broken links
  or wording that change no meaning are made without a note.
- We do not silently delete published pieces to hide an error. If a piece
  cannot be corrected, we say why at its address.

## Independence

Advertisers and newsletter sponsors have no say over what we write or how.
Sponsored material, when there is any, is labelled as sponsored. We do not
accept payment for coverage, links or reviews.
