HastCode explains how real systems are built. It is written for mid-to-senior
backend engineers who want to understand the architecture behind the software
they use and run: how it is put together, why it was built that way, and what
it costs.

## What we publish

- **Explainers** teach one architectural idea each. A Case Study reconstructs
  how a real, named system is built from its makers' public material; a
  Concept teaches one pattern; a Comparison sets alternatives side by side
  with a trade-offs table. Every Explainer opens with a TL;DR, includes at
  least one architecture diagram and ends with its sources.
- **Briefs** are short, dated commentary on something that just happened in
  tech, saying what happened, why it matters architecturally, and which
  Explainers go deeper. Briefs are curated from public sources; they are not
  original reporting.

Explainers are grouped into six Verticals: Distributed Systems, Databases &
Storage, APIs & Networking, Infrastructure & Cloud, AI/ML Systems, and
Languages & Runtimes.

## Who is behind it

HastCode is written and edited by the HastCode editorial team, which is
accountable for every piece published here. Every piece carries our house
byline, [Mira Vance](/authors/mira-vance/): one consistent editorial voice,
openly disclosed as a house name rather than a real individual.

## How we work

Every claim links a first-party public source, drafts may be written with AI
assistance but are always researched, verified and edited by the Author, and
every Explainer is reviewed at least every 90 days. The details are in our
[Editorial Policy](/editorial-policy/).

## How HastCode is funded

HastCode is free to read. It will be funded by display advertising and by
sponsorship of its newsletter. Advertisers and sponsors have no say over what
we write, and sponsored material is always labelled as such.
